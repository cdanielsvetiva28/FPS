# STORMLINE — Story Bible

> *"For forty years we argued about the weather. Then we built a machine to stop arguing,
> and went to war over who got to hold the remote."*
> — Elin Saro, *The Last Forecast*, broadcast 1

Working title: **STORMLINE**. A single-player campaign that alternates between the cockpit
and the ground, inspired by Ace Combat and Call of Duty.

---

## 1. What we took from the genre (and what we avoid)

Before writing, I looked at what makes each series' campaigns work, so this story borrows
the *techniques* but none of the plots.

| Series | What works | What we borrow | What we avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Call of Duty: Modern Warfare** (2007, 2019) | Multiple playable perspectives on one war; shocking set-piece moments; betrayal by your own side; a ticking clock | Two protagonists whose missions interlock; one or two "nobody saw that coming" moments | Nuke-shock for its own sake; a lone rogue ultranationalist villain |
| **Call of Duty: Black Ops** | An unreliable narrator; the framing device *is* the mystery | A framing device that recontextualises the story by the end | Brainwashing twists |
| **Battlefield 1** | An anthology of personal war stories; war as tragedy, not glory | Quiet human beats between missions; soldiers on both sides are people | Pure anthology: we keep one continuous plot |
| **Battlefield 3 / 4** | Interrogation framing; squad loyalty; the cost of following orders | Loyalty vs. orders as the core dilemma | Interrogation framing (done well already) |
| **Battlefield 2042** | Climate collapse as the backdrop of war | Weather as a political force | Faceless "no-pat" mercenaries |
| **Ace Combat 04 / Zero** | The war told by someone *watching* the aces; a legendary rival | An outside narrator; a rival ace who becomes something more | Documentary-interview framing |
| **Ace Combat 5** | Pilots who discover the war is a lie and go rogue | The "go dark" turn in Act 3 | A shadowy cabal on both sides pulling strings |
| **Ace Combat 7** | Superweapon set pieces; weather and terrain as obstacles | Storms you fly *through*; one massive structure as the final level | Drones and AI as the villain |

**Our unique hook:** the war is about the **weather itself**. A continent-spanning
weather-control grid was built jointly by both superpowers to stop climate collapse. Now
each side wants to use it as a weapon. Air missions are flown *inside* the storms it makes,
and ground missions fight over the towers that make them. Both halves of the gameplay are
about the same thing.

## 2. The world (2047)

### The Weatherline

In 2029, after a decade of crop failures and coastal floods, the two great powers did
something unprecedented: they built a machine together. **The Weatherline** is a chain of
312 atmospheric towers (**pylons**) running across the continent of **Veyra**. They seed
clouds, steer jet streams and break up hurricanes before they form. At its heart is
**the Spire**, a 2 km tower in neutral territory that coordinates the whole grid.

For eighteen years it worked. Harvests recovered. Coastal cities stopped drowning. Both
nations told their people it was *theirs*.

What nobody says out loud: the grid was never designed to run this long. Its control models
are drifting. Small "corrections" now cause bigger storms elsewhere. The machine is
quietly failing.

### The powers

**The Halvard Commonwealth** — *the player's side*
- A maritime federation of islands and western coastline. Old money, old navy, the
  continent's strongest air force.
- Democratic, proud and tired. Its leaders sold the Weatherline to voters as a Halvard
  triumph.
- Capital: **Port Adair**. Military: the **Commonwealth Air Arm** and the
  **Halvard Coastal Rifles**.
- Colours: navy and brass. Aircraft: sleek and expensive, designed for the long fight.

**The Sevrin Directorate** — *the enemy*
- A vast continental state to the east, run by an elected technocratic council (the
  Directorate). Younger, faster-growing and hungrier than Halvard.
- Built most of the Weatherline's pylons and believes, with some justice, that Halvard
  takes the credit.
- Capital: **Kessaran**. Military: the **Directorate Aerial Command** and the
  **Sevrin Line Infantry**.
- Colours: slate grey and signal orange. Aircraft: rugged and numerous, built for
  bad-weather flying.

**The Free State of Aurel** — *the ground both sides fight over*
- A small mountain republic between the two powers. The Spire stands in its capital valley.
- Officially neutral and custodian of the Spire. In practice, both powers' engineers run it.
- Aurel's people are the war's real victims, and its storm-chaser culture gives us the
  narrator.

### The inciting incident: the Merrow Storm

On 3 March 2047, a superstorm that should not have been possible forms in four hours and
destroys **Merrow**, Halvard's second city. 61,000 people die.

The Weatherline logs show a command issued from a Sevrin-run pylon cluster minutes before
the storm formed. Halvard's Minister of Defence, **Calder Wynn**, goes on television that
night: *"This was not weather. This was a weapon."*

Sevrin denies it. Nobody believes them. Within three weeks, the two powers are at war.

**The truth (revealed in Act 2):** that command was an automatic correction issued by a
failing grid. Halvard's own engineers warned Wynn a year earlier that the Weatherline was
destabilising. He buried the report, because admitting it meant admitting the
programme, his life's work, was killing people. Blaming Sevrin let him keep the grid running.

## 3. Characters

### Playable

**Flight Lieutenant Mara Okafor — callsign "WREN"**
- Commonwealth Air Arm pilot, Lantern Squadron. 29. Grew up in Merrow; her younger brother
  was there when the storm hit and is missing.
- Brilliant, reckless, funny on the radio and silent on the ground. She joined the war
  wanting revenge.
- **Plays:** all air missions. Arc: from revenge to responsibility. She will have to choose
  between an order and a person.

**Sergeant Teodor "Teo" Vance**
- Halvard Coastal Rifles, Pathfinder section: a forward air controller. His job is to get
  close to the enemy and call in air strikes. 34. A former Weatherline maintenance
  technician, so he understands pylons better than anyone in his unit.
- Calm, dry and protective of his squad. He's seen what the machine does up close and
  doesn't trust it.
- **Plays:** all ground missions. His voice on the radio is what connects him to Wren:
  he calls her strikes for half the war before they meet.

### Squad and wing

- **Captain Ruth Achterberg — "LANTERN LEAD."** Wren's flight lead and mentor. Steady and
  by-the-book. She'll follow orders to the end, and that is her tragedy.
- **Flying Officer Jun Sato — "LANTERN 3."** The youngest in the squadron. Chatty and
  superstitious about storms.
- **Corporal Dela Mbeki.** Teo's second-in-command: sniper, sceptic, the squad's conscience.
- **Private Owen Price.** A rookie rifleman on his first tour, and the player's mirror for
  what the war costs.
- **"SKYWARD."** The Halvard AWACS controller, the voice of command in every air mission.
  Sounds reassuring and follows orders exactly. Becomes chilling after the turn.

### The rival

**Colonel Ilse Varga — callsign "SABLE"**
- Commander of Sevrin's **Stormcrow Squadron**, the only unit trained to fly *inside*
  Weatherline storms using live grid data.
- The best pilot on the continent and a veteran of the grid's construction. She flew
  survey missions for the pylons.
- She knows Sevrin didn't cause Merrow. Her own command won't let her say so.
- Wren's rival for two acts, her reluctant ally in the third. The game's most important
  relationship.

### Antagonists (mirror images, not a conspiracy)

- **Minister Calder Wynn (Halvard).** Architect of the Weatherline programme. Not a
  monster: a man who can't let his life's work be the thing that killed Merrow. Every lie
  he tells makes the next one necessary.
- **Marshal Dravik Orlan (Sevrin).** Sevrin's hard-line commander. He has learned how to
  push the grid into deliberate storms and plans to use the Spire to fire one at Port Adair
  to end the war.

The two never meet or conspire. They are two powerful people making the same mistake
from opposite sides: *the machine will save us if we hold on to it hard enough.*

### The narrator

**Elin Saro**
- An Aurelian weather broadcaster and one of the Weatherline's original scientists, now
  retired. When the war begins she keeps broadcasting her nightly forecast on pirate radio
  from the mountains.
- Her broadcasts, *The Last Forecast*, play between missions as the game's interludes. They
  are part forecast, part war diary, part coded messages to people on both sides who want
  the truth out.
- **The framing twist:** it slowly becomes clear she isn't just narrating. She is the one
  who leaked the buried report, and the one guiding Teo and Wren toward each other.

## 4. Campaign structure

Sixteen missions in three acts. Each mission is tagged:

- ✈ **AIR** — Wren, arcade flight combat (Ace Combat style)
- ⛨ **GROUND** — Teo, first-person infantry (what we've built so far)
- ⇄ **COMBINED** — you switch between Wren and Teo within one mission, and what you do as
  one changes the other's mission

Between every mission: an *Elin Saro broadcast* (radio audio over a map/forecast screen).

### Act I — "Pressure Front"

**1. ⛨ "Merrow, After"**
Teo's Pathfinders search the ruins of Merrow for survivors two days after the storm. No
combat for the first half: flooded streets, rescue beacons, a world you understand before
you fight in it. In the second half, Sevrin special forces are found at a downed pylon,
trying to pull its data core. The first firefight.
*Teaches: movement, crouching, cover. Seeds: Teo notices the pylon's core logs were
already wiped, before the Sevrin team arrived.*

**2. ✈ "First Light"**
Wren's first sortie: intercept a Sevrin bomber wave over the strait. Learn to fly, lock
on, evade missiles. Ends with the first glimpse of the Stormcrows emerging from a cloud
bank, too fast and too low. They take out Lantern 4 and vanish.
*Teaches: flight, missiles, flares.*

**3. ⇄ "Voice on the Radio"**
The first combined mission. Teo's squad pushes up a coastal ridge toward a Sevrin
anti-aircraft battery. Every gun he destroys opens the sky for Wren's strike package
overhead. Switch to Wren for the bombing run, then back to Teo as her bombs land 200 m
ahead of him. They talk only by radio. Their first exchange:
> **WREN:** "Pathfinder, Lantern 2. Tell me where you want it."
> **TEO:** "About fifty metres further from me than you're thinking."

**4. ✈ "Eye of the Needle"**
Sevrin turns a Weatherline storm against a Halvard carrier group. Wren must fly *into* a
hurricane to destroy the Sevrin pylon steering it, through rain, lightning and blinding
visibility. Sable is inside waiting. The first duel, which neither wins. Varga on an open
channel: *"You fly well in the dark, Halvard. Ask yourself who turned the lights off."*

**5. ⛨ "Pylon 207"**
A night HALO jump onto a Sevrin-held pylon high in the Aurelian mountains. Stealth-first:
crouching matters, sprinting gets you heard. Teo plugs into the pylon to steal its logs.
What he reads doesn't make sense: *the Merrow command came from inside the Halvard
network.* He reports it up the chain. The reply: **"Delete it. Exfil."**

### Act II — "Low Pressure"

**6. ✈ "Long Way Down"**
Defend Port Adair from a massive Sevrin raid in a sandstorm the grid dragged in from the
interior. The capital's skyline, searchlights, a city in panic. Lantern Lead is shot down
and survives; Wren covers her parachute until she's picked up.

**7. ⛨ "Floodgate"**
Urban combat in **Tessaly**, a Sevrin border city the Halvard army is taking street by
street. The Weatherline has flooded half of it. Waist-deep water, rooftop snipers,
civilians in the crossfire. Price's first kill. Mbeki's line, which stays with Teo:
*"They're not the ones who made it rain."*

**8. ⇄ "Signal Fire"**
Teo, cut off behind enemy lines after Floodgate, finds a crashed Stormcrow pilot. It's
Varga's wingman, who dies telling him Sevrin's own Merrow investigation cleared them
months ago. Teo calls for extraction; Wren is the one who answers. Fly cover over a
burning forest while Teo carries the black box out on foot.

**9. ✈ "Clear Skies"**
A clear-weather mission: no storms, just the largest air battle of the war over the
Aurelian border. Pure dogfighting spectacle. Mid-mission, SKYWARD orders Lantern to strike
an Aurelian weather station. It's a civilian site, but "Sevrin is using it." Wren does it.
Afterward, Elin Saro's broadcast goes silent for one night. The station was hers.

**10. ⛨ "The Archive"**
Teo, Mbeki and Price raid the Weatherline engineering archive in occupied Aurel to recover
the Merrow records "before Sevrin destroys them." They find the buried report, signed off
by Minister Wynn's office, and the truth. The building is surrounded. Command's order to
Lantern Squadron: *destroy the archive.* Teo is still inside.

### Act III — "Storm Wall"

**11. ⇄ "Lantern Goes Dark"** *(the turning point)*
Played from both sides at once. Teo fights through the archive toward the roof with the
evidence. Wren, on her bombing run, hears his voice on the radio and realises where he is.
The player, as Wren, must **abort the strike against direct orders**, pulling up with the
release cue on screen. Lantern Lead rolls in to finish the job. Wren puts her own jet
across Achterberg's attack line, so close that Lead can't release without hitting her, and
the strike is called off. In that moment Wren becomes a deserter. Teo escapes; Price doesn't.
Achterberg on the radio, quietly: *"I'm sorry, Wren. I have my orders."*

**12. ✈ "Ghost Squadron"**
Wren and Jun Sato, who followed her, are hunted by their own air force. They fly through
Aurelian mountain valleys at treetop height, outrunning radar, toward a rendezvous Elin
Saro arranged. Waiting at the end, wingtips lit: **Sable.** The Stormcrows have deserted
too.

**13. ⛨ "The Broadcast"**
Teo and Mbeki escort Elin Saro to Aurel's old national transmitter so she can broadcast
the Merrow report to both nations at once. Halvard and Sevrin special forces both arrive
to stop her. For the first time, Teo fights *beside* Sevrin soldiers. The broadcast goes
out. It's not enough: Marshal Orlan has seized the Spire and begun charging a storm aimed
at Port Adair. Minister Wynn, rather than admit he lied, orders the Spire destroyed with
everything near it, including Aurel's capital.

**14. ✈ "Wall Cloud"**
A storm the size of a country is building around the Spire. Wren, Sato and the Stormcrows
must hold the air corridor open against both air forces so a ground team can get in. The
signature Ace Combat moment: a multi-phase battle inside a supercell with the whole sky
against you. Lantern Lead comes for Wren for the last time. You choose: shoot her down or
let her go. Achterberg's final line depends on it.

**15. ⇄ "The Spire"**
The finale, in two halves at once. Teo climbs the Spire on foot, floor by floor through
Orlan's troops and collapsing machinery, to reach the manual shutdown. Wren flies the
Spire's spiralling storm wall, destroying the emitter arrays one by one to keep the
structure from tearing itself apart while Teo is inside. Each array she destroys opens a
floor for Teo; each floor he clears takes an array offline for her. Orlan is in the
control room. Teo can end him or arrest him.

**16. ⛨ "Forecast"** *(epilogue)*
Six months later. The Weatherline is dark. The weather is wild and honest again, with
real droughts and real storms, and both nations are learning to live with them. Wynn is on
trial. Teo walks through a rebuilding Merrow. Wren is grounded, waiting for a court
martial that keeps getting postponed. Elin Saro's final broadcast plays. In the last scene,
Teo finds Wren's brother's name on a list of survivors evacuated inland the night of the
storm. He's alive. Wren gets the call. Cut to black on the sound of rain.

## 5. Themes

- **You can't win a war against the sky.** The Weatherline promised control over
  something that can't be controlled. Every side that tries to own it makes things worse.
- **Orders vs. people.** Both protagonists' arcs turn on one moment where the order is
  wrong. Achterberg is who they'd become if they followed it.
- **The enemy is also the victim.** Sevrin didn't cause Merrow. The player spends two acts
  fighting people who were telling the truth.
- **Honest weather.** The ending isn't a triumph. Turning off the machine means accepting
  hardship. The game's last note is hope, not victory.

---

## 6. Tone and style

- **Grounded, not grimdark.** Cockpit banter, squad humour, small human moments between
  the big ones (Battlefield 1's spirit).
- **Weather is the visual signature.** Every mission has a forecast; storms, fog, rain and
  sand shape both how it looks and how it plays.
- **Radio is the storytelling glue.** Most dialogue is radio chatter during play, so story
  never stops the action. Elin's broadcasts carry the quiet moments.
- **Two big player-choice moments** (Mission 11 is fixed; Missions 14 and 15 offer
  choices) that change lines and the epilogue, not the main plot.

## 7. What this means for the game we build

| System | Needed for | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Ground FPS | ⛨ missions | Mostly built: movement, crouch, weapons, AI, HUD |
| **Flight combat** | ✈ missions | New: arcade flight model, lock-on missiles, guns, flares, enemy aircraft AI, radar HUD |
| **Mission framework** | All | Mission scenes, objectives, checkpoints, win/lose, mission select |
| **Radio dialogue** | All | Timed voice lines/subtitles triggered by events; portrait + callsign on HUD |
| **Character switching** | ⇄ missions | Hand-off between a pilot scene and a ground scene in one mission |
| **Weather system** | All | Rain, fog, wind, lightning, storm intensity per mission (Godot volumetric fog + particles) |
| **Broadcast interludes** | Between missions | Map screen, subtitles, audio |
| **Save / progress** | Campaign | Mission unlocks and choices |

### Where Blender comes in

Blender is ideal for the things primitives can't do well: **aircraft**, vehicles, pylons
and the Spire. I can write **Blender Python scripts** that build these models
procedurally. You run them in Blender (Scripting tab → Run), they export `.glb` files, and
Godot imports them directly. You can then refine them by hand in Blender as much as you like.

### Suggested build order

1. **Flight prototype:** one jet, arcade controls, a few enemy fighters, missiles and flares,
   over a large terrain. This proves the second half of the game.
2. **Mission framework + radio dialogue:** objectives, triggers, checkpoints, subtitles.
3. **Vertical slice — Mission 3 "Voice on the Radio":** the first combined mission. If
   this mission is fun, the whole game concept works.
4. Weather system, then build out missions in story order.
